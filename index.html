Synthetic validation from the ACC abstract: ten CICU cases with zero, two or four planted deviations, eighteen in total.

I got eleven; it got eighteen, including all five image questions where it only heard my description. It won all seven disagreements. Small and not blinded, but the direction is hard to argue with.

If the machine passes the boards, what does passing prove?

Suppose we answer that it still lacks a certain je ne sais quoi. Then competence by passing the boards is no longer falsifiable.

Here's the deeper problem. Passing the boards is how we measure competence.

If a machine passes, and we say it's still missing a je ne sais quoi, then competence by passing the boards is no longer falsifiable. Either the exam can't bar incompetence, or a hooded examinee who excels gets barred anyway.

Either way it totally messes with what it means to be measurably competent.

We need a new paradigm for what competence means.
